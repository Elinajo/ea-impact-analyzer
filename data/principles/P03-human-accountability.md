# P03 — Human accountability

## Statement
Automated analysis may propose; a named human accepts. Accountability for a decision rests with a person, never with a system, and this applies in full to AI-assisted services.

## Rationale
Analysis that is fast, fluent and usually right is the kind most likely to be accepted without being read. The risk is not that an automated service is wrong — every method is sometimes wrong — but that its output is confident enough to stop the reader thinking. Where the tool is right nine times out of ten, the tenth case arrives with the same tone as the others, and there is no signal to distinguish it.

Requiring a named acceptor does not make the analysis correct. It keeps a person in a position where being wrong is visible and attributable, which is the condition under which people actually check. It also preserves something to point at afterwards: a decision with an accountable owner can be reviewed, and a decision produced by a service cannot.

This matters most where the automated output is an assessment rather than a measurement. A system can state which applications depend on another; it cannot state whether a change is safe to make, because safety depends on service levels, transition arrangements and operational risk that are not represented in any model it can read.

## Implications
Automated and AI-assisted services present findings as proposals, with the evidence they rest on, and identify what they could not determine. A service that cannot support a conclusion says what is missing rather than estimating.

Every proposal that leads to a change carries the name of the person who accepted it. Acceptance is recorded with the analysis, not separately from it.

Services are assessed on whether a reviewer can check their reasoning, not only on whether their answers are right.

## Applies to
Introducing a new AI-assisted service or automated analysis capability; any change to an existing service that widens what it decides rather than what it reports; impact assessments and architecture advice generated by tooling.
